# The Unfinished Tapestry

*The Hanging of Ashcombe, begun in the year 1382 and still being stitched.*

## What it is

An interactive fiction object: a very long wool-on-linen narrative embroidery of one fictional English village, Ashcombe. The village on the cloth is a live agent simulation. Its people wake, work, quarrel, court, marry, fall ill and are buried; fields turn with the real months; the river rises with rain. Everything that happens is stitched into the linen by an unseen needle. The hanging is never finished.

## Who it is for

Curious, patient visitors who arrive by link and stay to watch; people who return days later to see what changed. No accounts, no goals, no score.

## Mode

Experience. The work leads from the first viewport; the interface recedes.

## What must be true

- It is alive, not animated: autonomous, emergent, social, seasonal, persistent. A looping video could not replace it.
- Scroll is walking. The hanging runs horizontally; the visitor walks along it with any input.
- The visitor is stitched in as a stranger (a pilgrim) who is an agent in the simulation. The village may be suspicious, never cruel, and uses nothing personal beyond the fact and timing of visits.
- Real date sets the month's labour; real clock sets the day; the visitor's hemisphere turns the year the right way.
- Runs entirely in the browser as a static site: no database, no API, no runtime network.
- Ashcombe, its people and its hanging are fiction. Nothing is presented as historical fact. It is not a replica of any real hanging.

## Tone

Tactile, humane, funny and melancholy at once, the way village life is. Not a game, not a theme park, not parchment costume.

## Constraints

- Distinct from the sibling projects in this folder (*The Chronicle of Alderwood*: engraving on laid paper; *The Hollow Hill*: engraving, lacquer, brass, glass).
- Excluded typefaces: IM Fell, EB Garamond, Bodoni Moda, Old Standard TT, Pinyon Script, Cedarville Cursive, Special Elite.
- Plague is out of scope.
- WCAG 2.2 AA; pause control (2.2.2); designed reduced-motion presentation.
